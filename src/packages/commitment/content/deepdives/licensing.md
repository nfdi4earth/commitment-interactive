Why the commitment asks for open and permissive licences – and how to choose one.

## No licence ≠ open

If you publish data without a licence, others legally cannot reuse it, even if they can download it. A licence turns “visible” into “reusable”.

## Data licences

For research data, two licences are most widely used:

- **CC BY 4.0** – reuse with *attribution*
- **CC0** – public domain dedication

Many ESS repositories, e.g. [PANGAEA](https://www.pangaea.de/), default to CC BY 4.0.

## Software licences

**Permissive** licences (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD) allow reuse in almost any context. **Copyleft** licences (GPL) require derived work to stay open. See [choosealicense.com](https://choosealicense.com/) for a comparison.

## When open is not possible

Personal data, third-party rights or contracts can prevent open licensing. Document the reason and use the most open option that is possible (statement 8: “where possible”).
